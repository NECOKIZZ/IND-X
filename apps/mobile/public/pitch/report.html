The IndX thesis

Stock picking is the most popular skill game nobody keeps score of. IndX turns it into a weekly league on Solana. You build a basket of 3–10 tokenized stocks, lock it, and enter with a $5 ticket. When the week ends, every ETF above the median return wins the tickets of the ETFs below it, and every player gets their stocks back.

1 · The problem

Picking stocks is lonely, and the scoreboard is broken.

Millions of people have a view on which companies will do well. Today they can do one of three things with it, and none of them is a game:

  • Trade alone. A brokerage app shows your P&L against nothing. In a rising market everyone looks smart; in a falling one everyone looks wrong. Nobody can tell skill from the tide.
  • Bet on events. Prediction markets let you back a view on a single outcome, but not on a portfolio, and the money you stake is gone if you are wrong.
  • Play paper-money simulators. No stakes, no ownership, no reason to care.

Meanwhile the professionals mostly lose to the index: in 2025, 79% of actively managed large-cap US equity funds underperformed the S&P 500, up from 65% in 2024. Beating a benchmark is rare and measurable, which is exactly what makes a good competition.

The gap: there is no simple, fair, on-chain way to compete with a real portfolio, against other people, every week, where skill (relative performance) is what gets rewarded and your capital is never on the line.

3 · How IndX works

One round a week. Stocks in, stocks back.

WhenWhat happens
Fri evening → MonEntries open. Build an ETF: pick 3–10 tokens (stocks and funds, crypto capped at 20%, no token above 50%), set weights, choose an amount. IndX swaps your USDC into exactly that basket and locks it with a $5 ticket, in one approval. Or back someone else's ETF with a ticket.
Mon 13:30 UTCEntries close. The keeper records start prices for every token in the round.
Mon → FriLive. Standings move with the market. Each ETF's return is compared with the round's median return.
Fri 20:00 UTCSettle. End prices are recorded and the program settles on-chain. Winners are paid and every basket goes back to its owner automatically, with nothing to claim. The next round opens.

Rules that keep it fair

  • Same basket, same team. Two creators who build the same basket at the same weights join one team as co-creators, so nobody can split one idea into many entries.
  • Creators earn from backers. A creator earns 10% of what their backers win, and can set a fee of up to 2% when people buy their ETF.
  • Verifiable. The settlement inputs (prices, entries) are published, and anyone can recompute the result (/v1/rounds/:id/verify).
4 · The mechanism, in numbers

Pari-mutuel, weighted by how far above the median you finish.

IndX never plays against its users. Winners are paid only from the tickets of ETFs that finished below the median.

  1. Rank. Every ETF's return over the round is measured; the median return is the line.
  2. Pot. The losing tickets form the pot. A 10% take is removed: half goes to the platform, half to a season pot.
  3. Split. The pot goes to winning ETFs, weighted by accuracy a = 1 / (1 + r)6, where r is how far an ETF is from the best return relative to the median's distance. The best ETF has weight 1; one just above the line has about 1/64. A cap of 100× per ticket bounds any single payout.
  4. Thin rounds. If a pot is below 5% of winning stakes, the season pot tops it up.

Worked example: 20 ETFs, one $5 ticket each

10 finish below the median, so the losing stakes are $50. The take is $5 ($2.50 platform, $2.50 season pot) and the pot is $45.

Expected value per ticket: $4.75 on $5, a 5% take (the other 5% funds the season pot, which flows back to players in thin rounds). For comparison, a standard −110 sports bet carries about a 4.5% margin, and fantasy contests commonly take 10–15%.

5 · Why the median

Half the field wins, whatever the market does.

  • Market-neutral. The line is the field's own median, not zero. In a crash, the half that fell least still wins; in a rally, rising isn't enough. Only relative skill counts.
  • A hit rate people come back for. Roughly 50% of ETFs win every round. Top-N formats (top 10% wins) make 90% of players lose each week; the median keeps beginners in the game.
  • Skill still pays steeply. The accuracy weighting means the best ETF earns about 50× the last winner's gain. Picking well matters a lot more than barely clearing the line.
  • Capital never at risk. The basket is your ETF. It is escrowed and returned at settlement with all its gains or losses, exactly as if you had held it. Only the $5 ticket is at stake.
  • Hard to game. Identical baskets merge into one team, entries close before prices are recorded, and returns come from public price feeds.
For a player, IndX is holding a portfolio you'd hold anyway, plus a cheap weekly option on proving you picked better than everyone else.

10 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what we do about it.

RiskMitigation
Regulation. A paid contest on securities prices may be treated as gaming or as a securities product, depending on the country. xStocks are not offered to US persons.Launch where tokenized stocks are already offered; geofence; legal review before mainnet. The skill-based, pari-mutuel design (no house risk) is deliberate.
Price integrity. A bad price could pick the wrong winners.Multiple samples per phase; Pyth and Jupiter sources; published inputs anyone can verify; admin void with refunds if prices fail.
Smart-contract risk. The program holds stakes and baskets.Conservation invariants checked on-chain and in 55 tests; an external audit before mainnet; caps per round.
Liquidity. Large baskets in thin xStocks could slip.Server-side quotes with slippage headroom, a $10 minimum, and baskets sized by the player.
Cold start. A league needs players.A median format works from 4 ETFs; the season pot tops up thin rounds; creators earn from bringing backers.
